# Experimental generalized block Jacobi

This conventional FP64 solver has an experimental, opt-in SIESTA adapter; the standard solver remains the default. It is intended to remove the generalized eigensolver's internal host synchronization and scalar-download boundary so that an outer SCF convergence loop can remain on the device. The original local divide-and-conquer backend fails on retained Fe54 inputs. The opt-in 32-state batched-Jacobi backend now passes those inputs and five fresh Fe54 calculations; broad production tracing remains incomplete after a Fe16 device-access failure under CUPTI. Full-workflow residency is not established.

`native/block_jacobi.cu` reduces H Z = S Z W using a lower Cholesky factor, applies disjoint 32-by-32 or 64-by-64 local eigensolves in a block-Jacobi sweep, and back-transforms the sorted eigenvectors. Pair indices preserve global order. Padding uses a Gershgorin bound; convergence is normalized by the unpadded matrix norm. Real and complex inputs share the graph architecture. The lower triangle is authoritative and imaginary diagonal storage is ignored. A device conditional loop stops at relative off-diagonal norm below 2e-13 or records an error after the configured sweep limit. Device results distinguish invalid overlap, nonfinite data, local solver failure and exhaustion.

The plan owns its workspace and takes device pointers for input, output and status. Capture, allocations and initial staging occur before execution. The experimental checker stages three independently changing H/S problems, executes either three host-submitted solve graphs or a nested device loop over the problems, then exports all results. Continuing to the next independent problem after a failed solve is a checker reset test; a future SCF consumer must stop instead.

## Retained evidence

The CPU feasibility checks cover real/complex n=64,128,257 with dense overlap, n=576,1152 with diagonal overlap, and repeated/clustered spectra at n=128,129 with dense overlap condition 1000. These validate the mathematical construction only. Small local CUDA eigensolver run [20261006T040015Z](../results/20261006T040015Z/REPORT.md) passes 48 isolated real/complex checks across batch sizes 1,2,4,18 and changing matrices, including conditional capture and workspace poisoning. It does not validate the generalized blocked algorithm.

First blocked GPU attempt [20261006T042549Z](../results/20261006T042549Z/REPORT.md) compiles and retains all 77 source hashes. Complex n=18 executes three solves with maximum residual 3.744e-16, overlap-orthogonality error 6.182e-15 and eigenvalue error 2.665e-15, but 522 conditional-body kernel events have CUPTI correlation ID zero. The observer rejects phase attribution, and the component fails overall. Three unexpected failures stopped that run, leaving 33 scenarios unvalidated. Its independent SIESTA force/ablation comparisons and both ordinary/graph known-copy calibrations pass.

## Corrections tested in the second attempt

The plan now supplies persistent cuBLAS workspace and warms the final back-transformation, with exact call/line diagnostics on failure. NVIDIA documents that captured cuBLAS operations can otherwise create allocation/free nodes unsupported inside CUDA 12.6 child graphs; user-owned workspace is its recommended remedy ([cuBLAS CUDA graph support](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/12.5.0/pdf/CUBLAS_Library.pdf)). This is a diagnosis to test, not a claim that the failure is fixed. Unsupported captured node types fail explicitly.

The observer additionally retains graph/node identifiers as raw diagnostics. They do not by themselves identify a replay or phase; missing launch correlation still invalidates coverage. The checker now records numerical and residency gates separately and continues numerical scenarios after trace-only failures, while retaining a failed overall result. Three unexpected numerical/setup failures still stop the batch. No numerical threshold or phase-attribution requirement has been loosened.

Next gates include complete conditional graph trace attribution, real SIESTA matrix and end-to-end upstream equivalence, and integration with occupations, density, grid, Pulay and convergence. Forces/stress and geometry work remain separate GPU-migration requirements.

An observer-only experiment builds CUPTI 12.8.90 beside the original 12.6 observer. NVIDIA documents conditional-node tracing fixes in [CUPTI 12.8](https://docs.nvidia.com/cupti/12.8/release-notes/release-notes.html). Production CUDA/cuBLAS/cuSOLVER remain 12.6.3; only isolated blocked-solver workers select the alternate profiling library. The observer checks its build/runtime ABI, decodes the corresponding memcpy record version, and rejects unsupported batched copies. Ordinary and graph known-copy calibrations must both pass before any blocked-solver checks. Package/header/library/source hashes are retained.

## Second GPU attempt and controls

Graph inventories contain 199–202 nodes, no forbidden nodes and no host workspace. The corresponding nested workers fail with illegal device access, as does the independent two-replay conditional known-work calibration. Ordinary/simple-graph calibration passes and recorded build/runtime CUPTI ABI values both equal 26. Three unexpected numerical/setup failures stop the batch after six of 36 scenarios. This does not establish whether profiling, the conditional graph construction or another defect causes the device fault. The next controlled experiment runs the unchanged solver without an observer and then with the original CUPTI 12.6 observer, in fresh sequential processes on one GPU. No production solver has changed. Independent Fe2 force/control science and their original-observer CUDA/filesystem traces still pass.

The SIESTA adapter uses `REMAG_BLOCK_SOLVER=1` / `--block-solver`. It reuses a generalized solve graph at fixed dimensions, orders existing default-stream matrix assembly with an event, and retains the ordinary spectral/vector consumers. Setup uploads have a separate accounting boundary. The adapter still downloads a four-byte status after each solve and synchronizes its stream; it is a numerical integration experiment, not a scalar-free SCF implementation. `--block-solver-ablation` selects the unchanged cuSOLVER path as a direct control. All 36 untraced component checks are mandatory before using this adapter in the harness; the existing scientific gates and final-axis MAE checks are unchanged.

## Fe54 local nonconvergence in the whole-cycle path

The new device-cycle integration fails Fe54 in `20261006T083554Z` before any accepted iteration, then passes in `20261006T085932Z` without numerical changes. A repetition attempt [`20261006T090823Z`](../results/20261006T090823Z/REPORT.md) reproduces failure after 36 accepted iterations. Its new device record retains `info=22`, local pair 7, round 0, sweep 10 (zero based), in a real n=486 solve. NVIDIA defines positive `info` for this routine as nonconvergence of that local matrix ([pinned CUDA 12.6 documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/12.6.2/cusolver/index.html)). No solver or scientific tolerance has changed.

Raw failed H/S and the 272-byte blocked-solver record are retained. Lower-triangle-authoritative LAPACK analysis finds finite inputs, positive overlap with condition about 743, relative generalized residual 6.386e-15 and maximum overlap-orthogonality error 3.169e-14. The failed sweep's final global off-diagonal norm happens to be below the 2e-13 threshold, but local nonconvergence remains fatal; it must not be ignored to publish a successful solve.

The diagnostic now preserves the first failed local matrix in device storage, exporting it only after failure outside SCF. A replay checker executes eight copies of the exact failed generalized input in fresh sequential workers: flat graph, outer conditional loop, additional conditional guard, and guarded execution with private identity warmup matching production. These tests distinguish reproducible input failure from graph nesting/warmup effects; they are not yet qualified. Failed production rows retain unknown transfer totals when upstream abort prevents normal telemetry finalization.

The [exact replay](../results/20261006T091702Z/REPORT.md) reproduces the original error in all 32 solves, including both warmup choices and all nesting depths. The first failing 64-state local matrix has Frobenius norm 6.0533 and off-diagonal norm 1.0174e-14. Local LAPACK `evd` also fails on it, while `ev`, `evr` and `evx` solve it with residual below 8.0e-16. This rules out attributing the observed failure solely to CUDA graph nesting or a cuSOLVER-specific defect.

The experimental `--block-width 32` variant retains the same FP64 generalized construction, 2e-13 global stopping tolerance and 20-sweep production limit. CPU schedule checks pass with 11/14 sweeps for 64/32-state local problems. GPU run [20261006T092854Z](../results/20261006T092854Z/REPORT.md) passes all 36 solver fixtures, 32 whole-cycle fixtures and 32 replays of the original failure. Fresh Fe54 still fails after three accepted iterations with a different local 32-state problem (info 30, pair 3, round 12, sweep 6). Smaller blocks alone are not a fix. Neither failed run qualifies timing or cycle-transfer claims.

Both full generalized problems are retained in `benchmarks/solver-regressions/fe54-failures.npz`, with source hashes, failure context and lower-triangle-authoritative LAPACK eigenvalue references in `manifest.json`. The next opt-in backend, `--block-local-solver jacobi --block-width 32`, uses FP64 `DsyevjBatched` / `ZheevjBatched`, machine-epsilon local tolerance, sorted local eigenvectors and a 100-sweep local cap. It leaves the outer blocked and SCF gates unchanged. Its capture compatibility, numerical reliability, transfer scope and end-to-end behavior remain unqualified until tested; the default backend remains `syev` with width 64.


## Batched-Jacobi reliability evidence

Run [20261006T093950Z](../results/20261006T093950Z/REPORT.md) passes 36 solver fixtures, 32 composed-cycle scenarios, all 64 exact replays of the two failed generalized inputs and five fresh Fe54 calculations. Their same-allocation standard-GPU control also passes. Maximum replay residual is 7.022e-15, overlap-orthogonality error 1.827e-12 and eigenvalue error 1.987e-12. The five device cycles cover 270 accepted iterations with zero explicitly counted in-cycle host/device copies. Candidate wall-time median is 28.968 s (27.462–31.611 s), versus 38.943 s for the GPU control, a 1.344x ratio. Iteration counts vary 52/49/63/46/60 versus 57, and the control is measured once. The reused CPU reference cannot qualify a CPU speedup.

The subsequent CUDA/filesystem-traced batch `20261006T095252Z` passes seven production calculations before Fe16 non-collinear faults with an illegal device access and a reported Xid 31. Fe54 is not reached. Calibration success does not qualify the failed workload; absent final telemetry remains unknown. The same backend is being tested without CUPTI to separate numerical execution from observer effects. Neither the solver tolerances nor the scientific gates have changed. The default remains the old experimental 64-state `syev` backend until broader qualification; specify `--block-width 32 --block-local-solver jacobi` for the reliability candidate.
