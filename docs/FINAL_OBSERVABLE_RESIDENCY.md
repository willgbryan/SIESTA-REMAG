# Final observable residency: implementation history

**Current scope, 9 October 2026:** the supported production path computes final
forces, stress, density fields and population observables on the GPU, retains
electronic state, and gates H/D/E downloads on requested outputs or supported
host consumers. The [broad production qualification](../results/20261007T184850Z/qualification-summary.json)
and [current boundary summary](GPU_RESIDENCY.md#latest-accepted-checkpoint)
record the accepted implementation; [current progress](PROGRESS.md) records
later numerical and performance results. CPU geometry/setup and ionic work
remain. The dated stages below describe the migration history, not a current
list of unimplemented force consumers. Exhaustive residency certification is
not a delivery prerequisite under the [revised specification](SPEC.md).

At the initial sparse-state checkpoint on 6 October, the sparse owner exported
H/Hold/D/Dold/E/Eold after SCF because final force/stress and analysis still
consumed host matrices. Removing those exports required migrating their
consumers. A final download of requested scalar/force/stress output is a separate
boundary; it does not justify downloading electronic arrays to compute those
outputs on CPU.

In qualified sparse-state run `20261006T042922Z`, Fe128's three candidates take 55.248–62.419 s, with 26.415–26.601 s in PostSCF/FINAL_HF. The raw profiles assign 20.450–20.622 s to `dfscf`, 3.856–3.867 s to `nlefsm`, 0.284–0.285 s to `kinefsm` and 0.278–0.279 s to `overfsm`. At that checkpoint, final observables consumed roughly half the median end-to-end time. The same-allocation sparse-state control spends 26.425 s in FINAL_HF, including 20.445 s in `dfscf`; this work was on CPU in both variants. These are inclusive routine times, not additive exclusive component totals.

Upstream `final_H_f_stress.F` recomputes the final Hamiltonian from the converged output density, accumulates neutral-atom, kinetic, nonlocal/SOC, mesh and overlap contributions, and then combines force/stress arrays. The result is not just a re-export of the last SCF scalar record. Relevant consumers are:

| Consumer | Required resident data / additional staged geometry |
| --- | --- |
| `dfscf.f` | D, final Vscf, Vaux, atomic occupations, sparse periodic lookup, orbital values and first derivatives on mesh points, atom assignments and displacements |
| `PartialCoreOnMesh` / `NeutralAtomOnMesh` | Final potentials/density and geometry-dependent radial/core/neutral fields and derivatives |
| `kinefsm` | D and geometry-dependent kinetic integral derivatives |
| `nlefsm` / `nlefsm_SO_off` | D, projector overlaps/derivatives and SOC conventions |
| `overfsm` | E and overlap derivatives, including spin layout |
| Final analysis | Device charge/moment/band contractions plus only requested result exports |

`dfscf` is the first force target suggested by the measured profile. Its density contraction uses double-precision recomputed orbital values and gradients, unlike the single-precision phi cache used for the existing SCF grid contraction. Reusing that cache without equivalence tests would change the reference calculation. Preserve the spin-box Hermitian reduction for eight-component SOC and the factor of two on off-diagonal potential components. Its force contraction subtracts the atomic-density/Vaux term, then multiplies by twice the grid volume. Stress uses displacement times orbital derivative divided by cell volume, with upstream's tensor ordering.

A staged geometry/derivative representation can separate immutable basis evaluation from electronic contraction. Validate each contraction against the pinned upstream formulas, then retain density/potential/derivative data through final force computation. Component agreement alone is insufficient: displaced forces, stress, relaxation and SOC x/z calculations must pass end to end. Geometry updates invalidate all derivative/topology caches explicitly. Wider settings such as Harris forces, DFT+U, QMMM and extra corrections remain outside the current supported route and must not be silently approximated.

At the intermediate core/neutral checkpoint, GPU core, neutral and dfscf
contractions shared authoritative device accumulation, with orbital and
core/neutral derivatives generated on device. Integral/SOC/overlap and other
analysis consumers still ran on CPU. The next integral primitive and its
pristine reference are described in [TWO_CENTER_RESIDENCY.md](TWO_CENTER_RESIDENCY.md).
Those consumers were subsequently integrated into the supported production
path described at the top of this document. Complete independent residency
observation remains unqualified and is deferred under the revised goal;
conventional numerical agreement and measured speedup govern current delivery.

## Handoff after the device-cycle pilot

The production cycle now retains its final D/E, clustered electron density and full potential until explicit publication. Its initial bridge publishes all required legacy matrices and destroys the plan before CPU final observables. The next ownership change must extend that lifetime through final force consumers; simply keeping a pointer after publication would be invalid.

The final grid-force path additionally needs the sequential deformation density and Hartree potential from `GridPotentialPlan`. Upstream `Vaux` is the Hartree-plus-neutral potential without XC; its clustered form feeds `dfscf`, while partial-core work uses the diagonal-spin difference between full potential and Vaux. `NeutralAtomOnMesh` uses the spin-summed deformation density. Exposing only the final full potential is insufficient for these consumers. Preserve these mesh buffers and their immutable geometry owner while implementing the three grid-force contributions, then migrate kinetic/nonlocal/SOC and overlap/energy-density contractions. Only remove an export once all of its consumers have migrated and the full force/stress/relaxation gates pass.
